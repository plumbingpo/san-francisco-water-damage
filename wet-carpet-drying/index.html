Wet Carpet Drying in San Francisco

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In San Francisco, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
