Typical Water Damage Problems in Los Angeles

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Los Angeles and you will hear the same six answers. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Los Angeles. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: A long, heavy rain can overwhelm street drains and send water across the yard and into the house. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our San Francisco Restoration crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
