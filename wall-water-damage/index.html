Wall and Drywall Water Damage in San Francisco

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Warning Signs

  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
