Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

San Francisco Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying

Signs You Need Structural Drying in San Francisco

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose San Francisco Restoration:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.
