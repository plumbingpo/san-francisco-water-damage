How to Shut Off Your Water in an Emergency in San Francisco, CA

Home / Blog / Emergency Water Shutoff Guide in San Francisco, CA

When water is pouring into your home, shutting it off limits the damage faster than anything else. You could avoid extensive property damage and save thousands of dollars just by knowing where your shutoff valve is and how it works. Before an emergency ever happens, each homeowner in San Francisco, CA should learn where the valve is and how to turn it.

Locating Your Main Water Shutoff Valve

Check these spots in your San Francisco home for the main valve: along an interior garage wall, close to the foundation wall down in the basement or in a crawl space, inside the laundry area or a utility room, up front where the street line enters the house, or at the meter shutoff, which is in an access box at ground level near the street. The valve is typically a gate valve (round handle) or a ball valve (lever handle). A ball valve is the more reliable of the two and closes with a quarter turn.

The Quick Way to Stop the Water

A gate valve closes when you turn its handle clockwise, to the right, for several full turns, to the point where it will not go further. A ball valve is off when its lever sits perpendicular to the pipe, so turn it to that position. Never force a stuck valve, since it can break and leave you worse off. Go to the meter and close it there with a meter key or an adjustable wrench.

Individual Fixture Shutoffs

A separate valve on most appliances and fixtures lets you cut water to just that spot while the rest of the house stays on. Look for shutoff valves under sinks, behind toilets, behind washing machines, and near water heaters. Knowing these locations allows you to stop localized leaks quickly while maintaining water service to the rest of your home.
