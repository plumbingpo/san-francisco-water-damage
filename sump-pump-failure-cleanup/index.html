Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in San Francisco

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Do not think of it as clean water. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Boxes and Stored Items

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Coverage

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • The volume of boxes, furniture and other contents to sort, clean or dispose of.
  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.
